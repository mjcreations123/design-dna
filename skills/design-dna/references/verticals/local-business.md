## Serve the real customer journey

Identify what customers actually need to decide and do. Depending on the
business, that may include offer, audience, location or service area, access,
transport, hours, availability, price or qualification, menu or services,
dietary/allergen information, preparation, booking, contact, pickup,
delivery, cancellation, refund, or another policy.

This is an open content model. Include what the real journey requires; do not
create sections or routes to satisfy a local-business template.

Let routes and surface modes follow the business's actual customer journeys.
Do not assign a recurring home/menu/gallery/booking structure merely because
the subject is a local business.

The public entry should support the customer decision without requiring the
designer's explanation. Most service and visit contexts need quick recognition
of the offer and a useful next step. An experience-led venue or editorial
opening may unfold differently when orientation and continuation remain
intentional.

## Gather specific material

Ask for the highest-leverage evidence available, such as identity and signage,
place, product, service, people, menus, packaging, photography, neighborhood,
landscape, clientele, operating rhythm, voice, claims, and distinguishing
constraints. The useful set depends on the business.

If material is unavailable, create a clearly labeled, reversible concept. Do
not invent a founder story, address, hours, menu, sourcing practice,
accessibility feature, event, review, rating, amenity, availability, or policy.

Missing photos require an explicit media decision. Depending on the brief and
owner authority, use owner-supplied, commissioned, licensed, or generated
media, illustration, a capture plan, or a deliberately media-light direction.
Choose the amount, roles, and treatment the candidate needs. Record rights,
privacy, generated status, depicted claims, review, and fallback; do not let an
input gap silently choose the art direction.

Keep public concept labeling proportionate. Put missing-input inventories,
design rationale, detailed provenance, and readiness logs in internal records;
the customer-facing page should sound like the business.

## Direct without category costume

Build from the actual place, offer, audience, voice, service rhythm, material,
owner preference, and cultural context. Category conventions and historical or
fashionable references are allowed when they fit; they are not automatic
defaults or automatic defects.

Use exact selected-source/state/component mappings and observable transfer decisions. The direction may
be quiet, exuberant, familiar, decorative, utilitarian, editorial, image-led,
typographic, playful, historical, experimental, or something else. Do not
require a predetermined font, palette, route silhouette, media count,
interaction, time register, or removal test. Judge whether the rendered whole
is convincing for this business and meets the accountable owner's brief.

## Handle menus and service data

- Keep names, descriptions, prices, variants, availability, and
  dietary/allergen information distinct and owner-editable where practical.
- Do not communicate dietary, allergen, safety, or availability meaning by
  color or icon alone.
- Record when volatile information was confirmed and who maintains it.
- Provide print, low-bandwidth, translated, or alternate formats when the real
  audience and context need them.
- Make temporary unavailability and seasonal change maintainable.

## Implement integrations honestly

Do not activate maps, reservations, ordering, payments, email capture,
analytics, pixels, or cookie-setting embeds without approval and real
configuration. When a third party owns the transaction, explain the handoff
and preserve a safe return path.

For addresses, branch finders, service areas, pickup points, venue plans, or
directions, apply [location and wayfinding](../craft/location-wayfinding.md).
Provide a text/list path and verified details when a useful map fails or is
unavailable. Never present an invented pin or travel time as operating fact.

## Prove supported journeys

Test the journey branches the business actually supports. The following are
operational prompts, not page forms or required route architecture:

- **Visit, menu, retail, or pickup:** current hours, address, access,
  availability, price, depicted products, dietary statements, exceptions, and
  handoff.
- **Appointment:** eligibility, service choice, price or duration uncertainty,
  preparation, availability, confirmation, changes, cancellation, reminders,
  privacy, and third-party return.
- **Quote or service area:** location qualification, scope, authorized
  evidence, estimate limits, response expectations, escalation, and out-of-
  scope handling.
- **Urgent service:** genuine staffed availability, safety, response limits,
  location, hours, and approved alternatives.
- **Clinic or regulated service:** identity, credentials, jurisdiction, scope,
  risk, emergency boundaries, privacy, accessibility, and specialist review.
- **Event or venue:** date/time, capacity, admission, age/access limits,
  transport, accessibility, weather, changes, cancellation, and the meaning of
  the action.

Add, combine, or omit questions according to the real journey. Across relevant
conditions, review mobile reachability, glare, slow networks, one-handed use,
zoom, content variation, stale information, unavailable states, and recovery.
Do not imply a historical time register unless it is deliberate and supported.

## Release blockers

Block public release for unconfirmed contact details, prices, hours, policies,
legal or safety claims, unlicensed assets, fabricated proof, or an apparently
live transaction that is only a mockup. Record the owner and safe interim
treatment for every unresolved blocker.
