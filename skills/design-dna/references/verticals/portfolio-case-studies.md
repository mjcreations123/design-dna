# Portfolio and case studies

Use this for individual, studio, agency, architecture, photography, creative,
technical, and professional work collections.

## Build from the work

Inventory:

- project type, audience, context, constraint, contribution, process, and outcome;
- exact people, partners, clients, roles, dates, locations, tools, and credits;
- approved artifacts, working material, final media, and confidentiality limits;
- what can be shown publicly, described anonymously, summarized, or not used;
- the visitor's decision: assess craft, fit, specialty, process, or availability.

Let different bodies of work create the case-study depth, route structure,
media rhythm, and visual logic they need. Reuse structure when the viewer's
question and evidence match; do not force unlike work through one case-study
recipe.

## Represent contribution honestly

- State personal or team role and distinguish leadership, contribution,
  collaboration, direction, and implementation.
- Credit collaborators and licensed material according to approval.
- Use outcomes only with source, scope, timeframe, and owner.
- Distinguish shipped, proposed, exploratory, student, speculative, and
  confidential work.
- Do not fabricate client praise, process artifacts, metrics, awards, press,
  or recognizable private interfaces.
- Redact or recreate confidential material only with permission and unmistakable labeling.

## Tell the useful case

Choose the evidence that helps the intended viewer judge the work:

- the problem or opportunity in concrete terms;
- relevant constraints and rejected alternatives;
- consequential decisions and why they followed from evidence;
- representative process—not ceremonial sticky-note theater;
- final behavior or artifact in legible context;
- outcome, limitation, maintenance, and what changed after launch.

A brief project may need a concise treatment and a complex system may need
deeper navigation, but neither form is mandatory. Do not pad thin evidence
with invented depth or proof-shaped visual treatment.

## Shape the collection

Provide filtering or categories only when the portfolio supports a real
taxonomy. Keep project identity, role, date, and status scannable. Design image
crops, captions, alternate text, video controls, and low-bandwidth fallbacks
for the actual media. Preserve direct links and orientation inside long cases.

Make inquiry actions truthful about availability, service, response, privacy,
and next step. Avoid faux scarcity, fake booked calendars, or generic
"transform your vision" copy.

## Verify

Review every public credit, client name, logo, outcome, date, role, asset right,
caption, link, and confidentiality boundary. Test sparse and media-heavy
projects, portrait and landscape assets, missing imagery, long titles, direct
entry, keyboard, zoom, reduced motion, touch, sharing metadata, performance,
and the inquiry handoff.

Block release for misleading authorship, unauthorized client disclosure,
unlicensed assets, private data, fabricated outcomes, or an apparently live
contact path that has not been configured.
