# Place and community publications

Use this for an editorial, civic, visitor, cultural, or celebratory site about
a town, neighborhood, region, or community. Pair it with
[cultural-context review](../quality/cultural-context-review.md) when lived
identity or religion is materially central, and with
[route-family art direction](../craft/route-family-art-direction.md) when a
multi-route Range Study is part of the brief.

This reference governs authority, truth, cultural care, documentary status,
and current utility. It does not prescribe a page menu, visual genre, font,
palette, level of density, media style, or route count.

## Declare the publishing stance

State whether the work is official, institution-owned, independent editorial,
owner-authored, a visitor resource, a campaign, a prototype, or another
clearly described form. Identify the accountable publisher and whose
perspective the work represents.

Do not borrow the authority of a municipality, religious body, certifier,
school, archive, venue, tribe, or community through visual tone or ambiguous
copy. Use a concise independence or non-endorsement note when confusion is
plausible. Keep internal methodology and evidence-gap narration out of the
main story.

Define what a visitor should understand or be able to do and where current
authoritative information lives.

## Build a source and authority model

Gather the sources the publication's actual claims require. Depending on
scope, these may include:

- current government data with geography, measure, year, and retrieval date;
- primary records, archives, museums, libraries, indigenous or tribal
  authorities, and attributed historical interpretation;
- current first-party institutional sources;
- owner-approved or attributable evidence for community life;
- responsible agencies or operators for parks, roads, transit, access,
  safety, events, hours, schedules, certification, and other volatile facts;
- individually verified first-party, commissioned, or licensed media.

This is an open source model, not a minimum checklist. Include what the
publication claims and record explicit gaps. A municipal or institutional page
can contain reliable history and stale current details at the same time;
preserve retrieval dates and cross-check volatile claims.

Keep distinct historical periods and authorities legible. Do not collapse
indigenous history, settlement, migration, religious development, industry,
conflict, growth, and the present into one inevitable or triumphal story.
Attribute contested interpretation and name meaningful gaps.

## Represent a plural place with a clear perspective

A publication may have a strong editorial center. It should not silently
reduce the whole place to one institution, population, landmark, industry,
problem, or aesthetic.

When one community is central to the commissioned perspective:

- give its language, practices, institutions, work, learning, food, services,
  and ordinary life the depth the brief warrants;
- distinguish common practice from universal behavior;
- situate it within the wider civic, educational, environmental, commercial,
  and cultural place where relevant;
- do not invent identity percentages from datasets that do not measure them;
- do not invent resident voices, representative families, consensus,
  endorsement, access, or uniform observance.

Use the terminology and density the audience actually needs. Verify unfamiliar
language, transliteration, translation, sacred material, symbols, and customs
with appropriate authority. Cultural acceptance cannot be self-certified by
the producing agent.

## Let routes emerge from editorial questions

Derive route scope, structure, and treatment from the questions visitors bring
and the source material that can answer them. Each route needs a truthful
editorial or functional job, content authority, and ending or continuation
appropriate to that job.

Do not select from a recurring Design DNA route menu. Do not duplicate one
content sequence with new topics, pictures, type, and color and call it range.
Likewise, do not force difference merely to fill a quota when two routes serve
the same kind of reading or task.

For each route, use exact selected-source/state/component mappings and observable
transfer decisions:

| Route | Visitor or editorial job | Evidence and content dependency | Exact source/state/component transfer | Adaptation, authority, and status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  | composition, type, media, color, material, ornament, interaction, sequence, or another project concern |  |

Keep shared navigation meaning and operation predictable while allowing
route-specific expression. No restrained route, motion route, map route,
archive route, or other archetype is mandatory.

## Use media honestly and imaginatively

Documentary material may include current photography, archives, maps,
posters, ephemera, records, audio, or data graphics. Illustration, generated
media, typography, diagrams, and abstract visual systems may also belong when
their status and role are honest.

For each consequential asset, record source or prompt/input, creator, rights or
terms, attribution, retrieval or creation date, material edits and crop,
depicted claim, privacy and cultural review, generated status, approval, and
route use at the level required by the project. A collection page does not
establish one license for every item.

Do not present generated or staged media as documentary evidence of real
institutions, historical events, recognizable community members, worship,
public figures, minors, or specific current conditions. Owner-authorized
generated illustration or atmosphere is valid when it cannot reasonably be
mistaken for those claims and its cultural details pass review.

Maps and spatial graphics must disclose what they are for and where their data
comes from. When a map carries useful destinations or decisions, provide an
equivalent ordered list or text path. A purely decorative map should not imply
navigation accuracy.

## Keep visitor utility current

Separate durable orientation from volatile operations. For hours, access,
alerts, calendars, ticketing, certification, schedules, religious times, or
availability:

- link to the responsible current source;
- repeat facts only when useful, with scope and retrieval date;
- avoid promises of access, endorsement, availability, or operating status;
- make external destinations and authority clear;
- show contact or accessibility information only when verified and useful;
- label unavailable accessibility details or direct readers to the responsible
  contact.

When religious or community schedules affect a visit, explain the planning
consequence without inventing observance rules, times, hours, or community
uniformity.

## Review the publication

Review the exact route family and evidence for:

- publishing stance, authority, sources, retrieval dates, correction path,
  and claims;
- perspective, internal variety, terminology, translation, pronunciation,
  privacy, sacred material, and cultural acceptance;
- current fact versus historical record versus editorial interpretation;
- institution names, endorsements, access, schedules, and volatile details;
- media quality, documentary status, rights, privacy, crop, attribution,
  disclosure, and generated artifacts;
- direct routes, navigation, meaningful route differences, responsive
  behavior, mixed-direction text, preferences, and no-JavaScript utility;
- map or data alternatives, external-link clarity, visitor safety,
  accessibility, performance, and maintenance;
- owner, cultural reviewer, content maintainer, expiry, and re-review triggers.

Do not call the publication official, representative of everyone,
comprehensive, culturally accepted, current, or endorsed beyond the exact
authority and evidence recorded.
