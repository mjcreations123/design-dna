# Service journeys and handoffs

Use this when a user's goal crosses pages, products, organizations, teams,
support, third parties, or online and offline channels. Design the whole
journey rather than polishing one isolated interface.

When the scope includes material backstage systems, policies, owners, service
levels, cross-organization handoffs, or recovery dependencies, compile the
[service blueprint](../../templates/service-blueprint-template.md). Keep its
verified, observed, reported, hypothetical, and unknown states distinct; the
artifact does not prove that an operational promise works.

## Map the whole goal

Define the actor, scenario, desired outcome, expectations, constraints, and
evidence. Map what happens before, during, and after the digital interaction:
actions, questions, decisions, emotions, touchpoints, delays, and recovery.
Base the map on research, service records, support evidence, analytics, or
clearly labeled hypotheses rather than assumed behavior.

Include every channel that actually participates, such as web, app, email,
SMS, phone, paper, in-person service, delivery, and support. Channel parity
means an equivalent achievable outcome, not identical screens.

## Map delivery and ownership

For each material step, record:

- the user-visible promise, action, channel, and status;
- information, consent, evidence, identity, or reference that crosses a boundary;
- the receiving team, system, provider, or organization and named owner;
- backstage dependencies, eligibility, policy, timing, and expiry;
- failure, recovery, escalation, and alternate-channel behavior;
- evidence that the handoff and stated capability work.

Do not invent service levels, integrations, staffed support, data sharing,
approvals, or continuity that the service cannot confirm.

## Design a complete handoff

At a boundary, explain what happens next, why, who is responsible, what moves
with the user, what must be repeated, and how progress can be checked. Preserve
identity, context, entered data, attachments, consent, and reference numbers
only when authorized and technically supported.

Make external destinations and return paths clear. Provide a reference,
receipt, status route, expected next event, and correction or support path
where available. Avoid dead ends and vague instructions to contact another
team. When repetition is unavoidable, explain why and minimize it.

When a journey includes a support case, complaint, dispute, or appeal, also
apply [support, complaints, disputes, and
appeals](support-complaints-appeals.md).

## Model cross-boundary state

Use the states the service can prove, such as received, queued, processing,
waiting for user, waiting for third party, partially completed, completed,
rejected, expired, cancelled, or escalated. Distinguish submission from
receipt, receipt from processing, and processing from outcome.

Design for stale or conflicting status, delayed messages, unavailable
channels, lost references, repeated submissions, broken return links, changed
eligibility, partial transfer, third-party outage, and an unreachable owner.
State what is known, what remains uncertain, who now owns recovery, and the
safe next action.

## Keep continuity inclusive

- Preserve access needs, language, communication preferences, and reasonable
  context across handoffs when lawful and supported.
- Explain opening hours, time zones, response windows, physical access,
  required documents, and costs from authoritative service data.
- Provide equivalent confirmation and recovery for non-digital routes.
- Equip front-line and support teams with the same current status and
  explanation the user receives.
- Track handoff failures, repeated contact, abandonment, and avoidable demand
  as service-quality signals.

Apply [privacy, consent, and permission
guidance](privacy-consent-permissions.md) whenever information or authority
crosses a boundary.

## Verify and escalate

Walk representative users through the full outcome, including arrival,
handoffs, return, status checks, correction, cancellation, support, and
follow-up. Verify data continuity, reference numbers, notification timing,
permissions, ownership, and actual downstream state. Test unavailable
channels, third-party failure, stale data, delayed response, duplicate action,
language and access needs, mobile, keyboard, and assistive technology.

Validate consequential assumptions with users and operational evidence. Require
operations, policy, legal, privacy, security, data, accessibility, integration,
and external-provider specialists to approve their parts of the service. A
journey map or interface review does not prove an operational handoff works.
