# Imagery and illustration

Use this when photographs, product renders, illustration, generated imagery, video, or decorative media materially shape the result.

## Assign a job

Every prominent asset should do at least one job:

- show the real product, place, person, process, or outcome;
- establish atmosphere grounded in the project;
- explain structure or behavior;
- provide evidence;
- orient the user;
- support narrative sequence.

Do not add generic lifestyle imagery merely to fill a layout. If useful imagery does not exist, design an honest text-led or material-led direction instead.

## Direct a coherent set

Define before sourcing or generating:

- subject and factual boundaries;
- shot or frame list;
- camera distance, angle, and perspective;
- lighting, palette, contrast, and grading;
- environment, casting, clothing, and representation;
- crop families, focal point, and safe zones;
- background treatment and relationship to type;
- motion, loop, audio, and reduced-motion behavior;
- rights, attribution, disclosure, approval, and replacement status.

Consistency does not require every image to look identical. It requires
differences to serve content rather than reveal unrelated sources. For
documentary material, preserve truthful variance in light, weather, wear,
labels, people, local context, camera distance, and working conditions when
those details belong to the subject.

## Protect documentary credibility

- Review the set as a contact sheet as well as one asset at a time.
- Distinguish atmosphere from evidence; do not let pristine, palette-matched
  scenes impersonate real work, product, place, or outcome.
- Preserve ordinary residue and variation that the source actually contains.
- Do not add fake grime, damage, noise, film artifacts, asymmetry, clutter, or
  other staged roughness merely to appear handmade or ungenerated.
- When factual recognition matters and authentic material is unavailable,
  choose an honest diagram, text-led direction, or explicitly labeled concept.

## Work with generated media honestly

- Do not depict a real product feature, person, location, menu item, view, or event inaccurately.
- Label concept imagery when a reasonable viewer could mistake it for evidence.
- Keep prompts, source inputs, material edits, and approval status in the asset manifest.
- Inspect faces, hands, text, logos, geometry, shadows, reflections, perspective, and repeated artifacts.
- Do not imitate a living artist or proprietary campaign on request; derive principles from broader references.

## Design responsive art direction

- Preserve the subject, not merely the file, across crops.
- Use source dimensions and formats appropriate to rendered size and device density.
- Supply width/size hints and avoid downloading desktop media to small screens.
- Reserve dimensions to prevent layout shift.
- Provide a meaningful alt text, a caption, or an empty alternative according to purpose.
- Ensure overlaid text remains legible for every supplied crop.

## Review

Check authenticity, rights, factual accuracy, set-level coherence and natural
variance, crop quality, performance, contrast, alternative text, and behavior
when the asset is slow, missing, blocked, or replaced.
