# Imagery, illustration, and visual media

Use this when photography, product renders, illustration, generated imagery,
video, data graphics, or decorative media materially shapes the result.

This reference does not require photography, a shot count, a media hierarchy,
or a visual style. It requires deliberate art direction, honest claims,
appropriate rights, accessible treatment, and rendered quality.

## Choose the media logic

Media may provide documentary evidence, product recognition, explanation,
orientation, narrative, atmosphere, identity, affiliation, beauty, ornament,
or formal structure. A work may combine these roles or be intentionally
media-light. Decorative media does not need an independent information task,
but it still belongs to the creative logic and must not obstruct the
experience.

Record the consequential decisions in the candidate's `creative_logic`:

| Decision | Why it belongs | What should be observable | Truth or rights boundary | Adaptation or fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | project, aesthetic, editorial, cultural, audience, or production evidence | rendered role, relationship, sequence, crop, or response |  | relevant width, state, preference, loading, failure, or replacement condition |

Possible sources include approved first-party assets, commissioned work,
licensed material, owner-authorized generated media, illustration, data
graphics, a capture plan, or a deliberately type- or object-led direction.
These are options, not an ordered preference list.

When the subject or brief depends on material recognition, atmosphere, scale,
use, people, or place, verify that the chosen media approach actually conveys
what the visitor needs. “No photos were supplied” does not settle the design;
either obtain an authorized path, change the direction deliberately, or record
the unresolved dependency.

## Direct the set at the level it needs

Define whatever is consequential for this project, which may include:

- subject, depicted claim, and factual boundaries;
- image or sequence roles and content coverage;
- camera, perspective, light, palette, contrast, grade, material, or
  illustration behavior;
- environment, casting, clothing, representation, privacy, and consent;
- crop, focal point, safe area, background, and relationship to type;
- animation, video, audio, controls, poster, and reduced behavior;
- rights, attribution, disclosure, approval, expiry, and replacement state.

Do not complete a fixed art-direction checklist for its own sake. Different
sources may legitimately retain different light, weather, wear, labels,
people, camera distance, or material character. Coherence means the set's
relationships make sense for the work, not that every image looks identical.

## Protect documentary credibility

When media is presented as evidence of a real product, person, place, process,
event, condition, or outcome:

- bind it to its source, rights, date, edits, depicted claim, and approval;
- distinguish documentary evidence from illustration or atmosphere;
- preserve source variation and context that affect interpretation;
- do not fabricate, remove, or cosmetically regularize factual details in a
  misleading way;
- disclose material generation or transformation when a reasonable viewer
  could mistake the result for documentary evidence.

Do not manufacture “human” credibility with fake damage, clutter, grain,
asymmetry, mistakes, or other staged roughness. Authenticity comes from honest
status and fitting detail, not simulated imperfection.

## Use generated media within authority

Generated media is allowed when the owner authorizes it and its role,
representation, and disclosure are honest. It may be documentary-ineligible
for one project and entirely appropriate illustration for another.

- Do not depict a real feature, person, location, product, menu item, view, or
  event inaccurately while presenting it as fact.
- Preserve prompts or source inputs, material edits, approval, intended use,
  and replacement status at the assurance level required by the project.
- Inspect the final pixels at rendered size and, when needed, at source size
  for text, logos, anatomy, geometry, repetition, lighting, shadows,
  reflections, perspective, and cultural detail.
- Review the complete set for unwanted repetition, implausible uniformity,
  false continuity, inconsistent identity, and visual quality.
- Do not imitate a living artist or proprietary campaign; derive broader
  transferable principles from authorized references.

No generated-media prompt recipe or required shot trio applies globally.
Create the images and variety this candidate needs.

## Design responsive and failure behavior

- Preserve the intended subject or visual relationship across crops; do not
  assume one file or focal point fits every container.
- Match source dimensions, format, loading, and delivery to rendered use.
- Reserve layout space where loading could shift content or controls.
- Give informative media appropriate text alternatives; keep decorative media
  out of the accessibility tree; use captions when context or attribution
  belongs in reading order.
- Verify overlaid text for every delivered crop and relevant contrast mode.
- Define behavior for slow, missing, blocked, unsupported, or replaced media.
- Provide complete controls and alternatives for meaningful time-based media.

## Review

Review the rendered page and the set together. Judge project fit, visual
quality, sequence, crop, responsive behavior, truth, rights, privacy,
representation, disclosure, attribution, performance, alternatives, and
failure handling. Do not infer quality from provenance paperwork or infer
authorship from aesthetic traits.
