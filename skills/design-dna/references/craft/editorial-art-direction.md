# Editorial art direction

Use this when a publication, feature, report, journal, portfolio story, or
content-led experience needs a recognizable editorial point of view. Pair it
with the editorial vertical for publication models, trust, access, and
operational requirements.

## Establish the editorial stance

Define the story's subject, audience, purpose, evidence, emotional register,
and relationship between words and media, then use those criteria to qualify
references before any page treatment exists.
State what the reader should understand, feel, question, and remember. Name the
editorial qualities to protect and the qualities the work must not imply.

Copy a form from a qualified reference with the same reporting and reading task: investigation,
profile, essay, dispatch, interview, visual story, guide, review, data story,
reference, archive, or another truthful form. Do not force unlike stories into
one fashionable template.

## Give routes distinct silhouettes

Map each route to an exact reference observation and reproduce a coherent
publication family without making every route share the same hero, card grid,
or content width. Let a feature, news item, reference page,
issue opener, topic index, author page, and archive differ according to their
job, evidence, reading length, and entry context.

For each material route, record the selected rank/observation/hash and copy:

- opening move, information hierarchy, reading rhythm, and ending condition;
- relationship among headline, deck, byline, date, status, body, media, notes,
  related coverage, and actions;
- which composition rules remain recognizable across the publication;
- which elements may vary with the story and which are deliberate one-offs;
- behavior for very short, very long, media-rich, media-poor, corrected, and
  developing content.

Asymmetry, scale, density, silence, repetition, interruption, steady cadence,
and change of pace do not fail by category. Use them only when the route's
selected reference supplies their editorial, atmospheric, or compositional
role; variation and decoration are source decisions, not producer options.

## Commission and sequence media

Create a factual media brief, then qualify visual references before sourcing or
generating important imagery. Record
the editorial job, subject, moment, point of view, required truth, desired
specificity, orientation, likely crops, minimum resolution, rights, credit,
disclosure, and unacceptable implications.

Plan media as a sequence:

- identify the opening image or visual premise and why it earns that role;
- distinguish evidence, explanation, atmosphere, portraiture, documentation,
  data, illustration, and ornament;
- vary distance, scale, orientation, and pace when the material supports it;
- preserve meaningful edges, gaze, action, labels, and spatial relationships
  across crops;
- define fallbacks for missing, slow, blocked, or unsuitable media;
- keep captions and credits attached to the correct asset in every layout.

Do not use an image merely to fill a slot. Do not imply a real person, place,
event, product state, or documented moment with generated or unrelated media.
Verify quotations, rights, transformations, disclosures, and attribution with
the accountable owner.

## Direct typography and reading pace

Treat typography as editorial structure rather than a preset scale. Reproduce
the selected reference's measured relationships for display, headline, deck,
metadata, body, annotation, caption, quotation, data, and navigation after
proving they fit the publication's voice and actual content.

Test rhythm with representative long and short text, real names, dates,
numbers, links, headings, lists, captions, and translated strings. Protect
legible measure, spacing, hierarchy, zoom, text reflow, and user text-spacing
over an art-directed line break. Match the reference composition and metrics;
when its line break cannot survive truthful content or access, reject that
source or use another observed state rather than tuning from taste.

## Use emphasis without manufacturing evidence

Pull quotes, highlights, summaries, statistics, and callouts must preserve the
source meaning and status.

- Use only approved verbatim language as a quotation.
- Keep the speaker, source, date, and surrounding qualification when material.
- Do not splice fragments into a stronger claim or style ordinary prose as a
  testimonial.
- Label paraphrase, summary, estimate, opinion, analysis, correction, and
  generated text according to policy.
- Do not invent a dramatic sentence because the composition appears to need
  one; change the composition or request approved copy.

Decorative emphasis must not suggest that one unverified phrase is the
publication's authoritative conclusion.

## Re-art-direct for smaller and alternate surfaces

Mobile reproduces the selected reference's narrow editorial composition, not a
scaled desktop page. Verify
the opening sequence, crop, type relationships, media order, interruptions,
sticky elements, tables, notes, navigation, and ending. Preserve the editorial
premise while removing dependencies on viewport width, hover, simultaneous
columns, or fragile text wrapping.

Where supported, also review print, sharing previews, reader modes, embedded
contexts, landscape, zoom, translated text, right-to-left text, and reduced
data or motion. An alternate surface may use a different crop or sequence when
the meaning, credit, and evidence remain intact.

## Make the direction inclusive

- Preserve semantic heading order, landmarks, source order, and meaningful
  image alternatives.
- Provide transcripts, captions, descriptions, or equivalent access for
  material audio, video, charts, diagrams, and visual essays.
- Keep text and controls distinguishable under contrast changes, zoom, forced
  colors, keyboard use, and assistive technology.
- Avoid conveying sequence, authorship, correction status, or meaning through
  position, color, motion, or image treatment alone.
- Give readers enough control over autoplay, animation, sound, and
  concentration-heavy presentation.

Accessibility alternatives should communicate the same editorial purpose, not
serve as a lesser appendix.

## Review the finished story

Review the real rendered article and its neighboring routes with editorial,
art-direction, accessibility, and production owners. Confirm:

- the first impression matches the story and publication rather than a generic
  template;
- hierarchy, pacing, media, and ending remain coherent at representative
  widths and content extremes;
- captions, credits, quotations, links, notes, corrections, and disclosures
  remain accurate and attached;
- route variation feels related but not mechanically repeated;
- absent media, long text, translation, zoom, print, and reduced-motion states
  retain meaning and finish.

Record protected qualities as well as defects. Do not average a distinctive,
evidence-supported direction into neutrality merely because reviewers have
different personal tastes.
