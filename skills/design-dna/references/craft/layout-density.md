# Layout and density

Use this when spatial relationships, rhythm, density, or adaptive structure
materially affect the experience. It does not prescribe a grid, spacing scale,
container set, breakpoint count, section sequence, or amount of whitespace.

## Derive the spatial logic

Classify non-negotiable, inherited, negotiated, and open constraints. Then map
the relationships that must survive: order, proximity, comparison, hierarchy,
shared context, cause and effect, action and consequence, or another
project-specific dependency.

Record the relevant `creative_logic` and its observable decisions. A layout
may be systematic or idiosyncratic, dense or spacious, symmetrical or
asymmetrical, stable or transform across routes. Multiple grids, spacing
languages, or compositional regimes are valid when their relationships remain
understandable and their differences serve the work.

Tokens, grids, components, and container rules can make repetition reliable,
but they are tools rather than aesthetic obligations. Choose their scope from
the actual content and maintenance needs. A justified one-off does not have to
be normalized into a reusable recipe.

## Make density fit the situation

Density may respond to frequency, complexity, expertise, urgency, device,
reading mode, atmosphere, or deliberate contrast between moments. Do not
equate whitespace with quality, compression with sophistication, or uniformity
with coherence.

For task-led work, keep necessary controls, labels, state, and consequences
perceivable in the working context. For editorial, cultural, artistic, or
entertainment work, pacing may include silence, abundance, interruption,
collision, repetition, or spectacle when the audience can still enter and use
the experience. Ornament and spatial drama do not need a utilitarian excuse;
they need a coherent creative role and acceptable impact on access and
performance.

## Adapt when relationships change

Do not treat mobile as stacked desktop or wide screens as stretched mobile.
When available space, content, input, zoom, locale, or user settings change,
recompose whatever no longer works. What changes may include order, adjacency,
disclosure, crop, density, navigation, control placement, comparison method,
or the entire spatial premise.

Keep semantic order and operability intact. If the visual order differs from
source order, verify that keyboard, assistive-technology, reading, and action
sequences remain coherent. A breakpoint or container rule is justified by an
observed relationship change, not by a standard device list.

## Stress the real layout

Choose test states and widths from the declared support range and the risks of
the design. Include relevant combinations of:

- short, long, missing, translated, right-to-left, and unbroken content;
- sparse, typical, and worst-plausible data or media;
- zoom, text enlargement, text-spacing overrides, and forced colors;
- portrait, landscape, split-screen, short-height, and safe-area conditions;
- open navigation, menus, dialogs, validation, virtual keyboards, sticky
  regions, tables, and nested scrollers;
- touch, keyboard, pointer, hover, and no-hover input.

Inspect continuously around any transition where relationships change, rather
than checking only named devices. Fix structural causes of clipping, overflow,
order confusion, detached controls, or lost context. Do not hide overflow or
accumulate one-off patches merely to make a capture pass.

## Review without enforcing a house silhouette

Begin with an unprimed render review. Describe what the layout feels like and
how it behaves before applying motif labels. Then ask:

- Are the intended relationships and creative logic perceivable?
- Do repetition and variation feel authored in this project, including any
  intentionally plural system?
- Does density support the actual use, audience, and atmosphere?
- Do extreme content and supported environments remain complete and operable?
- Is a problem caused by a spatial ingredient, or by its execution,
  combination, or unsupported rationale?

Centered layouts, card fields, bento arrangements, sidebars, unconventional
overlap, huge type, tiny type, generous whitespace, and dense information can
all be valid. Report concrete rendered failures; do not reject an ingredient
because it resembles a familiar genre or another website.
