# Brand systems

Use this for identity-led sites, campaigns, products, sub-brands,
co-branding, or maintained systems that must remain recognizable while
allowing the range their real work needs.

## Establish authority

Inventory the approved identity source, version, owner, rights, geographic and
language scope, accessibility constraints, channel constraints, and review
date. Distinguish verified standards from patterns merely inferred from
existing work. Do not redraw, recolor, animate, generate, or otherwise alter a
protected asset without authority.

For an existing brand, inspect actual identity, content, photography, product,
place, campaign, and component material before proposing change. For a
greenfield concept, label provisional decisions and do not fabricate history,
values, recognition, endorsement, or market position.

## Separate inherited constraints from reference-open expression

Classify consequential decisions:

| Decision or field | Class | Authority and scope | Change owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | non-negotiable, inherited, negotiated, or reference-open |  |  |

Recognition may live in a name, mark, language, color relationship,
composition, sound, behavior, material, or another approved property. Do not
assume every brand has the same fixed-versus-flexible split.

Keep functional behavior predictable where consistency protects users:
navigation meaning, control semantics, states, forms, data, focus,
accessibility, consent, and recovery. This reliable foundation does not require
one type system, grid, component silhouette, palette, density, or expressive
grammar across every brand surface unless authority says it does.

## Map brand relationships

For master brand, sub-brand, product, campaign, partnership, sponsor, or
co-brand contexts, record what actually applies:

- which identity leads in each context and why;
- required marks, naming, hierarchy, spacing, and legal treatment;
- shared, negotiated, and reference-supplied expression fields;
- campaign lifespan, archive or sunset behavior, and ownership;
- localization, cultural, representation, rights, and market constraints;
- collision or legibility concerns when identities meet;
- who can accept an exception.

Never infer partnership, certification, sponsorship, access, or endorsement
from visual proximity.

## Record exact source/state/component authority

Use exact selected-source/state/component mappings and observable transfers rather than a producer-authored brand-style
taxonomy:

- route-manifest identity, selected observation bytes, mapped source state,
  census component, target-specific evidence, limits, and status;
- accepted recognition decisions and their scope;
- reference-open expression fields and who owns source selection;
- reusable functional and visual relationships;
- campaign-, route-, content-, or channel-local decisions;
- intentional one-offs and optical exceptions;
- relevant responsive, print, sharing, locale, preference, loading, and
  failure behavior;
- ownership, review triggers, lifecycle, migration, and deprecation.

| Decision ID | Decision | Reason and authority | What should be observable | Scope and adaptation | Status/owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |  | systemic, brand-local, campaign-local, route-local, one-off, or exception |  |

Typography, composition, crop, image treatment, color, voice, iconography,
illustration, texture, material, sound, motion, and interaction remain
available, but any field not supplied by the verified brand/system requires a
measured exact-fit reference before implementation. The producer does not fill
that gap. Do not require a brand to demonstrate every field or standardize
expressive work into one neutral shell merely because its values can become
tokens.

## Verify recognition, expression, and use

Test the relevant surfaces and conditions before freezing a maintained system.
The breadth depends on where the brand operates, how costly change is, and
which decisions remain uncertain; there is no universal surface count or
archetype set.

Review:

- whether required identity and legal treatment remain correct;
- whether intended recognition and expression survive the relevant content,
  route, channel, locale, and device conditions;
- whether variation is meaningful rather than random or cosmetic;
- whether shared behavior remains accessible, usable, and maintainable;
- whether local systems and exceptions retain their reason;
- whether co-brand hierarchy and claims remain truthful;
- whether the accountable owner accepts the result.

Do not require recognition with the logo hidden or any other removal ritual
unless that question matters to the project's own identity contract. Record
what evidence was tested, what remains open, and who owns future change.
