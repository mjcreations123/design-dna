# Evidence policy

Use evidence to improve judgment, not to construct an eternal blacklist. Runtime guidance contains the decision method; the canonical package keeps source cards and evaluation results outside the installed skill under `maintainer/`.

## Distinguish evidence types

| Type | Appropriate use |
| --- | --- |
| Standard, law, or official technical guidance | Establish an applicable baseline and its limits. |
| Formal research or web-scale data | Frame a risk, mechanism, prevalence, and counterevidence. |
| Expert practice | Supply a contextual craft heuristic, never a universal detector. |
| Platform documentation | Document a builder, framework, or product default at a particular date. |
| Community observation | Identify current perception and candidates for testing. |
| Owner policy | Apply the owner's explicit preference while labeling it as preference. |
| Internal behavioral evaluation | Show whether the skill changes outputs under documented test conditions. |

No single color, typeface, library, layout primitive, punctuation mark, or animation proves generated authorship.

## Change a risk rule carefully

Before promoting a nonstandard risk:

1. Create one source record per source.
2. Record the exact claim, locator, retrieval date, scope, limitation, counterexample, positive action, confidence, owner, and next review.
3. Require either two independent contextually relevant sources, repeated internal evaluation across projects and hosts, or a clear standard.
4. Keep individual social posts and vendor commentary as candidates unless corroborated.
5. Record source lineage and do not count a synthesis, its derivative catalog,
   and its upstream threads as independent corroboration.
6. Map every active risk to active evidence and prevent retired evidence from authorizing it.
7. Preserve rejected hypotheses so weak folklore does not repeatedly return.

Review standards, formal research, and stable official guidance at least every 180 days. Review community perception, platform defaults, and visual-trend observations at least every 90 days. A release audit should fail for overdue active evidence unless an explicit, dated exception is recorded.

## Evaluate visual candidates proportionately

Prefer clusters and causes:

- unexamined framework or generator defaults;
- unsupported content or proof;
- system incoherence;
- repeated first-pass structure;
- missing responsive and interaction work;
- category or cultural assumptions;
- cross-project repetition without reason.

Treat named motifs and font lists as expiring examples. Preserve legitimate brand, language, performance, usability, and accessibility exceptions.

## Record and retain responsibly

Use links and concise summaries instead of copied source material. Archive or hash sources when link drift would undermine verification. Classify records as public, internal, or confidential; redact before sharing; and retain screenshots or captures only for an owner-approved period.

Do not treat awards, stars, likes, pins, engagement, a tool vendor's marketing, or an agent's self-assessment as proof that a design decision is universally correct.
