# Cultural-context review

Use this when a community, religion, ethnicity, language, disability,
neighborhood, indigenous history, or another lived identity is central to the
site's subject or art direction. This review protects factual and
representational integrity; it is not a request to flatten culture into neutral
corporate language.

## Define the authority boundary

Identify:

- the accountable owner who commissioned or approves the work;
- the community or identities materially represented;
- the intended audience and whether the voice is insider, bridge, visitor, or
  institutional;
- an owner-authorized cultural reviewer with relevant lived or professional
  authority;
- the exact claims, terminology, imagery, routes, and build the review covers;
- relevant privacy, safeguarding, legal, religious, or institutional limits.

A producer may inspect sources and identify risks, but cannot certify their own
work as culturally accepted. One reviewer also cannot speak for every member of
a diverse community. Bind acceptance to the reviewer's authority and stated
scope rather than treating it as universal approval.

## Build the cultural source packet

Prefer first-party and primary material:

- accountable community or institutional guidance;
- current official information from named organizations;
- owner-approved language and pronunciation;
- records from credible archives, public agencies, or subject authorities;
- representative lived-language material supplied with permission;
- current operational links for changing schedules, access rules,
  certifications, and observance details.

For each material claim, record source, publisher, retrieval or review date,
scope, and expiry risk. Separate:

- verified fact;
- attributable perspective;
- common but non-universal practice;
- editorial interpretation;
- unresolved or prohibited statement.

Do not infer identity percentages, uniform beliefs, access, observance,
politics, family structure, ability, or community consensus from visual
evidence, anecdotes, surnames, or proxy demographic data.

## Choose a truthful point of view

State the publication's relationship to the community. An independent
celebration, insider essay, visitor guide, service site, and official
institutional source carry different authority.

Center the commissioned perspective honestly without implying that it is the
only life in the place. Show internal variety where it is material. Avoid both
token inclusion and a generic "everyone is the same" treatment that erases
real practice, language, time, place, or difference.

Explain unfamiliar terms where comprehension requires it, but do not turn
ordinary community life into spectacle or anthropology. Keep public copy about
people, places, practices, and useful context rather than about the producer's
research method.

## Treat language and sacred material carefully

Record preferred spellings, transliterations, capitalization, translations,
pronunciations when relevant, and terms that remain untranslated. Distinguish
linguistic review from broader cultural acceptance.

Do not invent:

- quotations, prayers, blessings, scripture, sacred text, or translations;
- religious rulings, certification, access, schedules, or observance claims;
- community slang or an insider voice unsupported by approved material.

Do not use sacred language as texture, crop it into meaningless fragments, or
place it where truncation, animation, reversal, or decoration changes its
meaning. When Hebrew or another right-to-left script appears, complete the
[localization and bidirectional review](localization.md) and a real-font
specimen; visual plausibility is not linguistic correctness.

## Review people and media

For documentary media, verify identity, place, event, date where material,
permission or lawful basis, usage rights, attribution, privacy, and crop.
Consider whether a photograph that is legal to reuse is still inappropriate
for the proposed context.

Use particular care with minors, worship, education, illness, poverty,
bereavement, private rituals, security-sensitive institutions, and people who
may not expect promotional reuse.

Generated or staged media must not masquerade as documentary evidence of real
people, real institutions, religious events, community practices, or history.
When an authorized illustration or concept image is useful, give it a clearly
non-documentary job, record its provenance and factual limits, inspect cultural
details, and apply the required disclosure and owner review.

Do not make clothing, food, architecture, language, or religious objects carry
the entire burden of cultural specificity. Connect media to real activity,
place, purpose, history, or service.

## Record cultural acceptance

Record the review in the direction and visual-review evidence:

- review status: `pending`, `accepted`, `rejected`, or
  `not-required`;
- stable owner and reviewer identities and relationship to the represented
  context;
- exact candidate or build ID;
- routes, media, terminology, facts, and interactions reviewed;
- review date and evidence reference;
- required changes, unresolved disagreements, and expiry or re-review trigger.

Only the accountable owner may authorize `not-required`, and the producing
agent may not set it on the owner's behalf. Record that authorization in the
evidence. `Accepted` means accepted for the recorded scope and build, not
permanently correct.

Reopen review when a material cultural claim, translation, image, institution,
voice, route premise, or build changes. A new font, crop, interaction, or
responsive order can also require review when it changes reading or meaning.

## Apply the release gate

When cultural or community identity is materially central, keep public release
blocked while cultural acceptance is `pending` or `rejected`. A source review,
lint pass, accessibility scan, producer self-review, or prior version's
acceptance cannot substitute.

Before release, also verify:

- public stance and non-endorsement language where needed;
- time-sensitive facts point to maintained official sources;
- terminology and mixed-direction text in representative browsers and
  assistive technology;
- media rights, privacy, attribution, factual status, and disclosure;
- narrow-screen crops, order, truncation, and interactions;
- contact, correction, or maintenance ownership for a durable publication.

If an authorized reviewer is unavailable, preserve the candidate as
provisional, state the unperformed review, and avoid publishing claims that
depend on cultural authority.
