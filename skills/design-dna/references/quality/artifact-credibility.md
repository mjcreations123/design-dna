# Artifact credibility and cumulative-pattern review

Use this after a complete render when the work represents a business,
organization, product, service, publication, or other public proposition, and
whenever an owner says a result feels AI-generated, overdesigned, synthetic,
like a portfolio exercise, or like an agent trying conspicuously not to look
generic.

This is a rendered-artifact review, not an authorship detector or a list of
forbidden ingredients. Large type, strong color fields, numbering, grids,
cards, borders, editorial devices, generated media, animation, or a maximal
composition can all be excellent. The finding is the observed relationship:
unsupported accumulation, repeated machinery, category or business
implausibility, visitor-irrelevant performance, or a producer fingerprint that
overpowers the project.

## Review the artifact without its defense

Open the final wide and narrow captures before rereading the direction
rationale, diagnostic vocabulary, scanner results, or implementation notes.
The first pass asks what the pixels communicate on their own:

- What is this, who is it for, and what can someone actually do here?
- Does it seem to be an operating public surface, an honest concept, or a
  designer's demonstration of a visual idea?
- Which details make the organization, offering, product, place, or
  publication feel materially real?
- What feels staged, synthetic, ornamental, implausible, or present mainly so
  the maker can show that it was designed?
- What would an ordinary visitor trust, question, ignore, or try next?

Freeze those observations before revealing the rationale. A fluent explanation
cannot repair an artifact that does not communicate the intended public
relationship by itself.

## Test public and business credibility

Judge the candidate against the public reality its nouns promise. A real
business site normally contains ordinary operational material alongside its
expressive moments: concrete offerings, product or service detail, support or
contact paths, decision-relevant limits, policies or ownership where
applicable, and navigation that follows visitor questions. A publication,
portfolio, campaign, or artwork has a different reality and should be judged
against that reality instead.

Do not manufacture a generic business checklist. Ask which ordinary questions
this specific visitor and category create, which routes or content answer them,
and whether the visual concept has displaced any of those answers. A bounded
demo may remain intentionally incomplete, but its fixture and unavailable
behavior must still support a plausible encounter rather than serving as
scenery around an art direction.

Use the counterfactual public-surface test: if the project rationale and design
record disappeared, would the rendered site still make sense as something this
organization would plausibly operate? If the answer depends on knowing the
maker's concept, reopen the public proposition or information architecture.

## Test institutional-scale claims without scale theater

When a brief asks for a global, category-leading, or institution-sized public
surface, test the public reality at that scale. A huge headline, a long route
list, invented large counts, a stylized world map, or a dense footer can signal
ambition, but none proves that the visitor can actually find, compare, decide,
get support, or continue through the breadth the organization claims.

Identify the ordinary depth that this exact category needs: for example,
coherent category architecture, genuinely useful product variation and
findability, decision-level detail, complementary services, regional or market
context when it is real, and support or policy paths that answer consequential
questions. Do not turn this into a bulk quota or a generic enterprise checklist.
The point is to test whether the organization has enough useful public topology
for its stated audience, not to fill pages with fictional departments or data.

For a concept or demo, never state an unverified market position as fact. Let
the scale come from connected, truthful scenario content and disclose the
fixture where an unavailable service, account, inventory, payment, location, or
operating claim could otherwise be mistaken for live reality. If the broad
visual proposition outruns the usable public surface, reopen the information
architecture, content model, or product/service depth rather than adding more
branding effects.

## Map cumulative intensity

Review the whole route and route family, not isolated attractive sections.
For each major encounter, note what claims the highest attention and how much
visual energy it demands relative to adjacent content. Then inspect the
sequence:

- Does every section attempt to be the hero, poster, manifesto, reveal, or
  signature moment?
- Is there enough perceptual contrast between orientation, ordinary reading,
  comparison, action, evidence, and expressive emphasis for the intended
  genre?
- Do size, saturation, motion, edge treatment, density, image treatment, and
  rhetorical compression remain at one unchanging intensity?
- Does the page become tiring or theatrical because nothing is allowed to be
  routine?
- Conversely, does excessive quietness hide hierarchy or make the offering
  feel unfinished?

There is no required quiet-section count, intensity curve, or maximum display
size. A deliberately relentless artwork may be right. The question is whether
the cumulative energy serves this visitor, subject, and occasion—or merely
demonstrates ambition.

## Find repeated design machinery

Name the dominant recurring relationship cluster in ordinary words. It may
combine type scale, hard or soft containers, color fields, numbered devices,
labels, arrows, cards, image crops, shadows, borders, transitions, section
openings, CTA grammar, or copy cadence. Inspect where it recurs across sections
and routes.

Repetition is correct when identity, task, content family, comparison,
navigation, maintenance, or an explicit project convention explains it. It is
a finding when materially different content is forced through the same
attention device, when a neutral motif starts implying sequence or data, when
the visitor can predict every section before reading it, or when the cluster
resembles the producer's unrelated work more than this project.

Do not fix a cluster by swapping its font, palette, radius, shadow, or icon
while preserving the same encounter. Remove, vary, subordinate, or replace the
earliest repeated relationship supported by the rendered evidence.

When the finding concerns recurrence across sites, use [visual-grammar
variance](visual-grammar-variance.md). Compare with actual authorized output,
not a hypothetical version of the candidate with another industry's nouns and
images. Category transferability is not a defect; unexplained repetition of an
observed producer grammar is.

## Audit containment and affordance monotony

Inspect the entire route without treating cards, panels, background fields,
buttons, or underlines as forbidden ingredients.

For each prominent surround, ask whether the content is genuinely independent,
repeatable, actionable, stateful, selected, or owned, and whether the boundary
communicates that fact. If ordinary paragraphs, facts, steps, products, and
calls to action all receive their own decorated box, test whether alignment,
spacing, order, or a rule would express the relationship more directly. Then
review the sequence: repeated cardification or backplates can remain a finding
even when each isolated component looks polished.

For each background change, identify the context, state, material, chapter, or
emphasis change it communicates. A new section alone is not a reason for a new
panel. For each prominent action, confirm that buttons perform actions, links
navigate, and underlines support recognition or a deliberate editorial
convention. Look for a whole-site habit in which every primary action becomes
the same filled rectangle and every secondary action becomes the same
underlined phrase.

Record the relationship and its cause. Do not solve monotony by banning all
containers, flattening every field, removing useful underlines, or inventing a
new opposite template.

## Review type, copy, and media as systems

### Typography and composition

Inspect whether one display posture dominates every message regardless of
importance. Repeated oversized headlines, compressed line breaks, isolated
one-word lines, compact labels, or high-contrast blocks are findings only when
their accumulation flattens hierarchy, turns ordinary information into a
campaign slogan, or creates a recognizable producer trick. Preserve the
treatment where its meaning and context justify it.

### Public copy

Look for language that performs the design concept instead of serving the
visitor: portfolio captions, self-congratulatory aphorisms, invented editorial
systems, unnecessary sequence announcements, and headings whose main job is to
make a module look impressive. Review the repeated rhetorical machine across
the site. Keep genuine brand voice, useful persuasion, atmosphere, and
editorial expression; remove construction-facing narration and portable
showcase language.

### Media

Inspect the set for commercial and documentary plausibility, not merely
artifact-free pixels. Generated or stock media can look polished while still
feeling statistically generic through uniform light, immaculate surfaces,
anonymous people, unlabeled objects, impossible product details, repeated
camera distance, or imagery that never encounters the real operating context.
Determine whether each image provides recognition, evidence, atmosphere, or
orientation and whether its truth status supports that role. Do not use
illustrative generated media as factual proof of an actual product, facility,
person, customer, or outcome.

## Keep reviewer authority honest

Record the reviewer relationship and prior exposure. Producer self-review is
useful for revision, but it cannot become independent perception, target-user
validation, owner acceptance, or proof that the work no longer looks
AI-generated. When no independent or accountable reviewer is available, run
the artifact-only pass anyway and label the result `self-reviewed candidate`.

An unprimed reviewer should see only the exact rendered artifact for the first
observation. Do not explain the brief, intended style, previous criticism, or
suspected defect first. Record what they actually infer, including
disagreement. A reviewer saying that the site feels like a design exercise,
synthetic concept, or conspicuous anti-template attempt is direction evidence,
not a superficial preference to rationalize away.

## Close or reopen the direction

Bind the review to exact final wide and narrow captures and record:

1. the credible-public-surface result;
2. the dominant recurring device or relationship cluster;
3. the cumulative intensity and ordinary-work result;
4. business or category completeness relevant to this scope;
5. media credibility and synthetic-pattern result;
6. portfolio, process, and rhetorical-machine result;
7. cross-project visual-grammar result or the honest no-comparator limitation;
8. container/backplate result;
9. link/button/underline affordance result; and
10. reviewer relationship, limitations, and disposition.

Use `keep`, `revise`, `reopen direction`, `reject`, or `blocked`. A material
failure in public credibility, cumulative intensity, repeated machinery, or
media truth cannot close because the site is coherent, responsive, accessible,
distinctive, or technically clean. Revise the observed cause, rerender the
affected whole route or route family, and repeat the artifact-only observation.
