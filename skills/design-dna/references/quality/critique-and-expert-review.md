# Critique and expert review

Use this to structure design critique, heuristic review, or a consequential
direction decision. Do not collapse critique, usability testing, specificity
review, and owner acceptance into one vague approval.

## Contents

- [Name the review type](#name-the-review-type)
- [Prepare the room](#prepare-the-room)
- [Record perception first](#record-perception-first)

## Name the review type

| Review | Question it can answer | What it cannot prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design critique | Does the work fulfill its stated intent with coherent craft? | task success with representative users |
| Heuristic or expert review | Where does the design conflict with known interaction or content principles? | population prevalence or user preference |
| Usability test | Can represented users understand and complete defined tasks in the tested conditions? | universal access, market success, or visual authorship |
| Specificity review | Does the result rely on repeated generic clusters rather than project material? | whether AI was used |
| Owner acceptance | Does the accountable owner accept the exact rendered candidate for the stated scope? | specialist compliance or untested production readiness |

## Prepare the room

Record scope, maturity, objective, non-goals, exact build, routes/states,
requested questions, target audience, constraints, presenter, facilitator,
recorder, and decision owner. Give reviewers the rendered work, not only the
rationale.

When group anchoring could distort the question, use an individual unprimed
pass before discussion and invite dissent. Other review formats are valid when
they fit the participants, access needs, decision, and available evidence. Keep
the concept explanation separate from first-impression evidence when the
question depends on unprompted perception.

## Record perception first

Before showing scanner findings, risk codes, direction rationale, or previous
feedback, capture unprimed evidence that answers the actual review question.
For task-, service-, and product-led work, that may include whether people can
identify the offer, audience or situation, relevant state, and available
action. For narrative, art, editorial, cultural, or entertainment work, it may
instead test whether the invitation, orientation, continuation, and intended
unfolding are perceptible without prematurely revealing the full meaning.

A five-second exposure, first-noticed report, scan account, emotional response,
memory prompt, think-aloud, task attempt, comparison, or open description is
an optional method only when it can answer the declared question. Do not run a
fixed perception battery or demand immediate comprehension from every genre.
Ask what feels specific, confusing, excessive, unfinished, or worth protecting
only where those questions are relevant, and preserve surprising observations
that do not fit the producing team's vocabulary.

Anchor actionable feedback to route, state, viewport, screenshot artifact, and
region or bounding box. Separate observation, impact, possible cause, and
suggested correction. A preference without rationale may still matter when it
comes from the accountable owner, but label it accurately.

## Turn feedback into decisions

Invite deliberate dissent; do not average incompatible opinions into a neutral
compromise. The named decision owner protects concept coherence while
dispositioning evidence.

For each accepted finding, assign region or system cause, owner, due date,
protected qualities, correction, rerender target, and verification. Reopen the
direction when the problem is conceptual; use a bounded tuning pass when the
direction remains sound. Owner rejection remains pending until the exact
revised build is rendered and reviewed.
