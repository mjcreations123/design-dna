# Content discovery and minimum source packet

Use this before art direction when the supplied material is sparse,
contradictory, generic, or mostly aspiration. The goal is the smallest truthful
packet that can support specific decisions, not a long intake ritual.

## Assemble the minimum packet

Collect or explicitly mark unavailable:

1. **Audience and moment:** who arrives, from where, with what prior knowledge,
   urgency, device conditions, and accessibility needs.
2. **Primary job:** the decision, task, or understanding the surface must
   support and the observable success condition.
3. **Real nouns:** actual products, services, places, people, objects, data,
   terminology, categories, and constraints.
4. **Truth set:** approved facts, claims, prices, dates, policies, availability,
   capabilities, credentials, and their sources and owners.
5. **Proof:** approved work, product behavior, documentation, imagery,
   testimony, process, or results that can genuinely support credibility.
6. **Voice:** representative owner, customer, user, expert, support, menu,
   catalog, documentation, or product language.
7. **Identity material:** approved marks, type, color, photography, interfaces,
   packaging, place, artifacts, or established system decisions.
8. **Operational reality:** maintained content, states, integrations,
   handoffs, responsible owner, launch state, and known limitations.

Do not require every item before a reversible concept. Do require the facts
that make a public claim, consequential action, live integration, or
irreversible direction safe.

## Extract useful texture

From approved material, make concise lists of:

- subject-specific nouns and verbs;
- meaningful distinctions, sequences, thresholds, and exceptions;
- phrases the audience actually uses;
- concrete constraints and tradeoffs;
- sensory, material, spatial, temporal, or procedural details;
- repeated support questions and decision blockers;
- terms that must remain exact;
- clichés, generic promises, and imported category language to avoid.

Use these lists to shape headings, taxonomy, route order, interaction, media,
density, and component roles. Do not sprinkle unusual nouns into an otherwise
generic structure and call the result specific.

## Classify every source

| Status | Meaning | Allowed treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approved | Accountable owner permits current use in the named scope. | Use within its scope and review date. |
| Supplied, unverified | Present but truth, rights, or currency is unresolved. | Use only internally or label clearly; seek owner review. |
| Illustrative | Deliberately fictional or sample material. | Make scenario status unmistakable and avoid authority-shaped presentation. |
| Restricted | Confidential, personal, licensed, or research-bound. | Minimize access and follow its handling rules. |
| Missing | Necessary content is unavailable. | Omit, defer, or use an obvious reversible placeholder. |
| Contradicted | Sources disagree materially. | Preserve the conflict and obtain an accountable decision. |

Freshness, popularity, or polish does not convert a source into approval.

## Build bounded scenario content

Use this only when the project is explicitly a fictional sample, demo, or
prototype, or when an accountable owner approves illustrative scenario
content in a named scope. It is not permission to invent client facts.

1. Freeze the approved premise, audience, primary task, and every fact that
   must remain unavailable.
2. Create one internal content fixture that marks each entry `approved`,
   `scenario`, `pending`, or `prohibited`. Use the same fixture when comparing
   directions so better writing does not masquerade as better design.
3. Add enough clearly labeled scenario content to make the experience credible
   and testable without padding it with unsupported proof-shaped detail. The
   form and amount may include representative items or services, descriptions,
   categories, visitor questions, action outcomes, states, or other content the
   chosen experience genuinely needs.
4. Derive voice from the subject's nouns, verbs, setting, sensory qualities,
   and audience relationship. A distinctive voice can be illustrative without
   inventing a founder story, customer quote, or institutional authority.
5. Use exact sample prices, dates, durations, quantities, or availability only
   when the interface genuinely needs them. Mark them as scenario values in
   the internal fixture and make their illustrative status clear wherever a
   reasonable visitor could treat them as operating facts.
6. Keep public concept or sample disclosure proportionate and place it where a
   reasonable visitor needs it. Do not repeat caveats in every section, make
   the site narrate its own design process, or satisfy a universal count.

Never synthesize history, addresses, named real people, credentials,
testimonials, awards, partnerships, measured results, live status, documentary
events, or local detail as scenario texture. Those are proof-shaped claims, not
harmless flavor.

Generated concept media needs its own explicit authorization. The portable
publisher policy does not grant it. Authorization may come from an explicit
current instruction by an accountable owner or an active project-local owner
policy whose exact scope allows the named fictional sample or demo. Provenance,
artifact inspection, proportionate disclosure, and the ban on documentary
impersonation still apply.

## Work when evidence is sparse

- Reduce the number of public claims and proof-shaped modules.
- Choose a direction that works with modest real material.
- Use structure, proportion, typography, interaction, and honest utility
  instead of invented backstory or testimonial theater.
- Label concept identity, sample data, and unavailable integrations where a
  reasonable visitor could mistake them for reality.
- Keep decisions reversible until the missing source arrives.
- Record which missing input would most improve the next iteration.

Separate a missing fact from a missing expressive asset. A missing address,
price, review, or product capability cannot be invented. Missing photography,
however, requires a deliberate media decision rather than an automatic
photo-free layout or an automatic substitute-photo workflow. Approved
first-party material, commissioned or licensed media, an owner capture brief,
owner-authorized generated concept media, illustration, diagrams, typography,
sound, motion, objects, or another medium may be the right answer. These are
concurrent possibilities, not a preference order. Record the chosen medium's
authority, rights, truth status, inspection, access, delivery, fallback, and
any disclosure the actual use requires. Generated media must not impersonate
documentary evidence.

For a physical, sensory, spatial, hospitality, retail, food, product, or event
experience, record which qualities and visitor decisions the experience needs
to make perceptible and how the chosen media strategy will do so. Material,
atmosphere, scale, use, people, process, detail, sound, movement, or another
project-specific quality may matter; none forms a universal shot list. Do not
let missing input choose the art direction by accident. Abstract media,
typography, or elaborate interface treatment may genuinely become the
project's strongest medium when the creative logic supports it; review the
rendered result rather than treating any of them as a second-choice fallback.

Keep the source-gap log internal. The public page may need proportionate
concept or availability disclosure where confusion could occur, but it should
not repeat missing facts, design methodology, or unresolved-input language
until those caveats become the main content.

Do not fabricate specifics, precision, local detail, quotations, or proof to
cover a source gap. Stock media and decorative complexity may be valid creative
materials, but they must not impersonate missing project evidence or appear by
accident as compensation for it.

## Resolve contradictions

Record the conflicting statements, source, owner, date, and affected surface.
Use the safest non-misleading treatment while awaiting resolution. Do not
silently pick the source that makes the design easier or more persuasive.

## Completion check

Before direction, confirm that the packet can answer:

- What makes this project recognizably itself?
- Which user question determines the route order?
- Which claims and assets are safe to publish?
- Which real constraints should shape the system?
- What remains a concept, placeholder, or release block?
- Who can maintain time-sensitive content after handoff?

Record the useful answers in the direction brief. Retain links and concise
extracts rather than unnecessary copies of confidential source material.
