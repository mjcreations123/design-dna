# Decision case studies

Use these to see how project evidence can produce different decisions. They
are not starter themes, route menus, palettes, component kits, or a required
reasoning format. If the material or owner changes, the result should change.

## Persuade: neighborhood coffee counter

**Supplied material**

- A narrow contemporary counter near a commuter station.
- Morning service is fast; afternoons are slower and food-led.
- The owner supplies current menu data, stainless work surfaces, a blue awning,
  stamped pastry bags, staff photographs, and verified access information.
- No founder story, reviews, awards, or sourcing claims are approved.

**Possible derived decisions**

- Give current hours, station-side location, and the two service rhythms the
  prominence their customer value requires.
- Let the supplied awning, work surfaces, packaging, menu, and photographs
  inform visual relationships without requiring literal imitation.
- Let visit, menu, and pickup information take the structures their different
  questions need rather than cloning one route body.
- Keep the time register present-day because the real place and owner evidence
  support it.

**Rejected reasoning**

A complete café aesthetic and sentimental community story were chosen before
the supplied place, operations, and claims were considered. The result could
serve unrelated businesses and would impersonate missing evidence. Its
specific ingredients are not globally prohibited; the unsupported substitution
is the failure.

**Useful proof**

Test the customer conditions most likely to expose the direction: morning
one-handed use, current hours, price changes, unavailable items, glare, slow
network, address copy, directions handoff, and long allergen labels.

## Experience: city-block sound essay

**Supplied material**

- Approved observations cover first train, school dismissal, dinner
  deliveries, and late-night street cleaning.
- Captions and written notes exist; recordings and exact locations are not yet
  cleared.

**Possible derived decisions**

- Let the approved temporal material determine sequence and changing density.
- Use timestamps and transcript relationships while keeping unavailable audio
  and locations honestly pending.
- Preserve chapter entry, deep links, readable transcripts, and a complete
  reduced or static path.

**Rejected reasoning**

The interface added time-based spectacle and evidence-like graphics unrelated
to available material. Those moves are not inherently wrong; here they imply
recordings that do not exist and turn attention from the place to the producer's
default presentation.

**Useful proof**

Review the chosen sequence with media absent, motion preferences, direct entry
into later material, keyboard and touch, short height, and slow resources.

## Operate: equipment inspection queue

**Supplied material**

- Field inspectors work on shared tablets in bright light.
- They review assignments, record condition, attach authorized photos, flag
  unsafe items, sync later, and hand off to a supervisor.
- Status vocabulary, permissions, offline rules, and representative content
  extremes are supplied.

**Possible derived decisions**

- Prioritize assignment, sync, safety, recency, and next action according to
  the field workflow.
- Let wider and narrower containers use different task-preserving
  relationships rather than shrinking one composition.
- Make offline drafts, conflicts, partial uploads, permission, correction, and
  supervisor review complete states.
- Derive specificity from real objects, language, relationships, and working
  conditions while preserving familiar accessible controls.

**Rejected reasoning**

A generic monitoring interface spent space on proof-shaped summaries and
activity signals that did not help inspectors complete or recover work. That
diagnosis concerns the task mismatch, not a global ban on dashboards,
visualization, spaciousness, or animation.

**Useful proof**

Test relevant touch and keyboard use, zoom, glare, long identifiers, data
volume, offline capture, duplicate sync, conflicts, failed media, role changes,
and safe recovery.

## Read: municipal tree-care handbook

**Supplied material**

- The corpus contains seasonal procedures, species notes, safety warnings,
  tables, diagrams, definitions, revision dates, and responsible departments.
- Residents and trained crews arrive with different questions.

**Possible derived decisions**

- Separate public guidance from crew procedure while preserving shared terms
  and cross-links.
- Build navigation from the questions and source material rather than copying
  the organizational chart.
- Give warnings, prerequisites, definitions, tables, and revision metadata
  stable semantic treatment.
- Let the real diagram language, terminology, hierarchy, and local document
  material carry the chosen reading direction.

**Rejected reasoning**

A generic promotional-publication shell made the source harder to use and
weakened authority. Promotional and editorial forms remain allowed elsewhere;
the defect is their mismatch to this reference job and corpus.

**Useful proof**

Test direct links, search, print, copy, citations, wide tables, missing figures,
translation, RTL, zoom, screen reader, stale guidance, and urgent-warning
comprehension as applicable.

## Transfer the reasoning

For a new project, create its own `creative_logic` and observable decision
record:

| Decision | Project evidence | Expected observation | Adaptation or limit | Status/owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |  |  |

Record enough evidence and proof to support the real decision. Do not copy a
case's palette, type, route order, motif, motion, media role, or number of
steps because its reasoning was persuasive. Do not treat a case's rejected
move as a global ingredient ban.
