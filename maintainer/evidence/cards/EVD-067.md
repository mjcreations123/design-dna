# Expression can improve attention when it preserves recognition and function

## Claim

Purposeful visual expression may improve attention, emotion, and usability,
but only when category recognition, interaction conventions, accessibility,
and the core task remain intact.

## Observation

Google reports extensive mixed-method research on Material 3 Expressive. Its
examples show faster attention to prominent actions, but also show usability
loss when familiar structures become unrecognizable or labels disappear.

## Scope and limitations

These are Google-reported studies of its own system and products. They do not
establish that Material components, vibrant styling, large buttons, or any
named expression tactic belongs in unrelated work.

## Counterexamples

A banking workflow, dense operations console, quiet memorial, or established
brand may correctly choose restraint. A visually exciting treatment that harms
recognition or function remains a failure.

## Positive action

Use project-derived color, shape, size, motion, containment, or other means to
direct attention and create feeling; test the actual task and first impression;
retain recognizable interaction and accessible labeling.

## Validation

The implemented surface demonstrates the intended attention and emotional
effect without slowing task completion, removing needed labels, or importing
Material's house style.
