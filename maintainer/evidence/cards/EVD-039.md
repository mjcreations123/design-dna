# Hebrew needs script-aware layout and font verification

## Claim

Hebrew content cannot be validated by treating it as mirrored Latin text; its
direction, punctuation, emphasis, line behavior, and optional diacritics need
script-aware rendered review.

## Observation

The W3C Hebrew Layout Requirements editor's draft describes Hebrew as a
right-to-left, non-joining script and records typographic considerations for
slant, bold emphasis, spacing, punctuation, and niqqud. It notes that vocalized
text may require a font with clear mark placement and a different size.

## Scope and limitations

This is a technology-agnostic editor's draft and work in progress. It does not
choose a font, validate a translation, authorize sacred text, or replace
testing with native readers, browsers, screen readers, and real content.

## Counterexamples

A short borrowed Hebrew term inside an English sentence may need an isolated
inline direction rather than an entirely RTL page. A Latin transliteration is
not automatically preferable to an approved Hebrew form.

## Positive action

Declare language and direction at the smallest correct boundary, preserve
logical source order, isolate mixed-direction runs, choose fonts with the
required glyph and mark coverage, and obtain language and cultural review for
meaning.

## Supports

`RISK-TYPE-001`, `RISK-RESP-001`

## Validation

Review mixed English, Hebrew, numerals, punctuation, links, wrapping, copy and
paste, selection, zoom, narrow widths, fallback fonts, screen-reader output,
and any niqqud with a qualified Hebrew reader.

## Retention

Public W3C editor's draft; retain a dated summary and recheck within 90 days or
when a new working draft, gap analysis, or status is published.
