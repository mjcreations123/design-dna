# Fast first impressions include complexity and category recognition

## Claim

An unprimed first view can reveal whether a surface's visual complexity and
recognizable category posture support or undermine its intended encounter.

## Observation

Two experiments reported that visual complexity and prototypicality influence
website aesthetic judgments at very short exposures, with both factors
remaining relevant as viewing time increases.

## Scope and limitations

The study is from 2012 and concerns screenshot ratings, not complete modern
journeys, culture, accessibility, interaction, or every form of expressive web
work. It does not authorize a universal low-complexity aesthetic.

## Counterexamples

A festival, game, archive, or art experience may deliberately unfold before
its category becomes literal. A familiar transaction may benefit from high
prototypicality, while a campaign may validly challenge it.

## Positive action

Freeze an unprimed first impression and ask what category, stakes, and next
action the surface suggests before revealing rationale; revise only when the
observed misclassification harms this project's intent.
