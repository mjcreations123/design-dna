# Productive friction challenges defaults without becoming a house style

## Claim

For underspecified AI-assisted website work, context elicitation and deliberate
review can challenge probabilistic defaults. The mitigation is a process for
restoring intentional choice, not a list of prohibited visual ingredients.

## Observation

The paper maps an end-to-end vibe-coding lifecycle in which a model fills gaps
in an abstract prompt and a user may accept the first plausible output. It
proposes productive friction as deliberate pause, reflection, clarification,
and negotiation, plus contextual anchoring in existing identity material.

The authors use minimalist whitespace, standard sans-serif typography, and a
predictable hero as one illustration of a global average overriding a Japanese
retail context. That example identifies contextual mismatch in an aggregate;
it does not make any ingredient a universal detector or ban.

## Scope and limitations

This is a March 2026 preprint: a sociotechnical risk analysis and proposed
mitigation framework based on 13 academic sources, 50 grey-literature sources,
and walkthroughs of six tools. It is not a controlled comparison of generated
websites, an authorship detector, a prevalence estimate, or evidence that the
proposed interventions improve outcomes. The authors explicitly call for
future empirical work on real creator experiences.

## Counterexamples

A familiar layout, framework, font, palette, or interaction may be the strongest
answer when it fits the local audience, inherited system, and task. A bounded
change can reasonably follow one approved direction. Added friction can also
become needless burden when the important context is already known.

## Positive action

Ingest available project and cultural context, surface consequential
assumptions, clarify ambiguous intent, and challenge the first plausible
default with proportionate alternatives or counterevidence. Render, review,
and revise when visual evidence will improve the decision. Do not impose a
universal concept count, proof count, font list, palette, layout, or style ban.
