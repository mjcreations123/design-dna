# Composite widgets need a complete semantic and interaction contract

## Claim

A custom composite widget must fulfill the semantics, state, focus, keyboard,
pointer, touch, and announcement promises of its role and be tested in the
project's supported browser and assistive-technology pairings.

## Observation

The W3C APG supplies patterns for common widgets and explicitly describes
itself as informative, illustrative rather than prescriptive, and not a
production-ready design system. Its Read Me First requires interoperability
testing before production use.

## Scope and limitations

APG examples are not normative requirements, exhaustive valid implementations,
or copy-paste components. Browser, mobile/touch, and assistive-technology
support gaps can change the viable production pattern.

## Counterexamples

A native control can be more robust than a custom ARIA recreation. A genuinely
different interaction may depart from an APG example while still satisfying
the normative ARIA and accessibility contract.

## Positive action

Prefer native semantics. When a custom composite is necessary, map it to the
closest current APG pattern, record the full promised behavior, implement it,
and test the actual supported pairings.

## Supports

`RISK-UX-001`

## Validation

Exercise entry, exit, keyboard navigation, state changes, dismissal, focus
return, names, roles, values, and announcements on the critical route and
record the exact pairings and limitations.

## Retention

Current public W3C guidance. Review within 90 days and retain the informative,
non-production-ready-example limit.
