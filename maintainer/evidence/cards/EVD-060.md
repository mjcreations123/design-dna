# Public derivatives need safe names, validated content, and bounded originals

## Claim

Asset publication needs defense in depth: safe public filenames, validated and
rewritten content where appropriate, reinspection, and separation of restricted
originals from public delivery.

## Observation

OWASP's current upload guidance covers filename safety, content validation,
image rewriting, public retrieval risks, storage outside the web root, and the
data-leakage risk of sending private files to external scanners.

## Scope and limitations

This source governs upload security, not visual craft, consent law, licensing,
or a universal media transformation recipe. Design DNA adapts only the asset-
publication mechanisms; high-risk upload systems still need security review.

## Counterexamples

A repository-authored static icon does not need an upload threat model. A
rights-required filename or provenance credential may need deliberate
preservation rather than random deletion.

## Positive action

Keep private originals out of public roots, choose a safe derivative name,
rewrite or sanitize with a documented tool when risk warrants it, inspect the
result again, and avoid leaking client material to unapproved scanning services.

## Supports

`RISK-ASSET-001`, `RISK-SEC-001`

## Validation

The exact public derivative passes type, content, metadata, visual, and filename
review appropriate to its risk; storage and access boundaries match its
classification.

## Retention

Current public OWASP guidance under CC BY-SA 4.0. Review within 90 days and
defer full upload-security assurance to a specialist.
