# Feature availability does not establish product compatibility

## Claim

Browser support must be an explicit project contract, and feature-availability
data cannot replace critical-path tests across materially different supported
engines, operating systems, devices, or assistive-technology pairings.

## Observation

MDN's current Baseline definition covers availability across major desktop and
mobile browsers while explicitly excluding accessibility, usability,
performance, security, older-device, web-view, and assistive-technology proof.

## Scope and limitations

Baseline and browser-compatibility data change with browser releases. An
availability badge also does not identify a particular project's audience,
version policy, fallback promise, or real-device risks.

## Counterexamples

A controlled enterprise kiosk may support one browser. A public service may
need older devices and a no-script fallback. A mobile camera flow may require
real hardware even when every API reports broad availability.

## Positive action

Derive a browser/engine/OS/device matrix from the product contract and audience,
screen consequential features against current compatibility data, exercise
materially different supported critical paths, and disclose untested rows.

## Supports

`RISK-UX-001`, `RISK-RESP-001`, `RISK-CODE-001`

## Validation

The completion record names exact engine, browser, OS, real-versus-emulated
condition, build, route, and state. One Chromium pass never becomes an all-
browser claim.
