# Audit findings need confirmed evidence and explicit guards

## Claim

A trustworthy design audit distinguishes confirmed findings, contextual review
candidates, and unknowns; it does not silently convert a review into a redesign
or hide the false-positive guards that shaped its result.

## Observation

The pinned MIT UI-design skill requires exact file-and-line evidence, limits
shape-based claim detection to defensible scope, documents false-positive
exclusions, and treats ambiguous full-codebase evidence as unknown.

## Scope and limitations

Its React, Next.js, and Tailwind assumptions and context-free numerical or house
style rules are not portable. Listing non-findings can also become noise unless
the audit is consequential and the examples demonstrate a real contextual
guard.

## Counterexamples

A narrow copy fix does not need a catalogue of everything the reviewer chose
not to flag. A rendered visual relationship may be valid evidence even when no
single source line establishes it.

## Positive action

Bind source and rendered findings to exact evidence. Use `unknown` or a review
candidate when proof is insufficient, rerun the originating check after a fix,
and record a short set of deliberately rejected plausible patterns for
consequential audits.

## Supports

`RISK-UX-001`, `RISK-COPYFORM-001`

## Validation

An audit fixture should prove that known exclusions prevent plausible false
positives, that unknown is not rewritten as failed, and that a correction is
accepted only after the originating evidence is rechecked.

## Retention

Pinned public MIT practitioner guidance. Review within 90 days and preserve the
evidence-over-guessing boundary.
