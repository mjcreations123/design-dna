# Shared components, product recipes, and one-offs need different homes

## Claim

Reusable foundations, recurring product compositions, and justified one-off
experiences should be named and governed differently instead of forcing every
page through either one universal template or unrestricted custom code.

## Observation

Brad Frost distinguishes context-agnostic design-system components,
product-specific reusable recipes, and one-off snowflakes. He advises that
product-specific work move into the core system only when broader reuse is
demonstrated.

## Scope and limitations

This is an expert practitioner's vocabulary and architecture, not a standard
or controlled study. The labels are optional, and a one-off is not
automatically distinctive, usable, maintainable, or worth its cost.

## Counterexamples

A one-page brochure may not need three layers. Ten snowflakes that duplicate
the same behavior under different names are still repetition, while one
well-designed recipe may appropriately serve several related routes.

## Positive action

Classify shared foundations, route-family recipes, and true one-offs; record
why each belongs at that level; and promote a local solution only after real
reuse appears.

## Supports

`RISK-SYS-001`, `RISK-REPEAT-001`

## Validation

Review imports, markup, rendered silhouettes, and maintenance ownership. Flag
identical recipes disguised by surface tokens and unjustified one-offs that
duplicate a solved interaction.

## Retention

Public expert guidance; retain a dated summary and review within 90 days.
Treat the vocabulary as a reasoning aid, not mandatory product architecture.
