# Clear copy methods remain contextual to audience and genre

## Claim

Visitor copy should put useful meaning early, prefer concrete and direct
language where it clarifies the task, and account for audience and context
without forcing every project into one documentation voice.

## Observation

Google's official developer documentation guide recommends clear, concise,
conversational writing, active voice when it is clearer, second person for
reader actions, global-audience awareness, and sentence case as a documentation
default. The landing page reports `Last updated 2026-04-27 UTC` and states that,
except where otherwise noted, page content is licensed under CC BY 4.0 and code
samples under Apache 2.0. The guide also names contextual exceptions rather
than treating those choices as authorship rules.

## Scope and limitations

This is primarily technical-documentation guidance. It does not supply the
voice for a restaurant, campaign, arts publication, luxury brand, cultural
project, legal notice, or every interface. Its methods are adapted after the
project's approved facts, genre, language, and voice are known.

## Counterexamples

Passive voice can be clearer when the actor is unknown or irrelevant; first or
third person can fit institutional or editorial writing; an approved brand can
use title case, fragments, idiom, or an unusual cadence intentionally.

## Positive action

Resolve audience, source facts, genre, terminology, and voice first. Draft from
the reader's real decision, then use clarity techniques selectively and review
the copy in its rendered hierarchy.

## Validation

Run a facts-and-voice check, heading-only read, read-aloud pass, portability
test, and rendered wrap/hierarchy review. Confirm that the pass preserves
intentional voice and never treats punctuation or sentence shape as proof of
authorship.
