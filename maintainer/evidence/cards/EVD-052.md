# Contextual anti-slop editing should preserve facts and voice

## Claim

A useful post-draft copy pass names observable producer patterns, preserves the
client's facts and voice, and makes the minimum effective edit instead of
claiming to detect authorship.

## Observation

The pinned public MIT-licensed skill emphasizes voice preservation, no
fabricated claims, specific evidence, limited edits, and a portability test for
filler that could move unchanged to another company.

## Scope and limitations

This is practitioner guidance, not an empirical detector or universal writing
standard. Pattern catalogs become a recognizable anti-slop dialect when they
turn every punctuation mark, sentence opening, cadence, or rhetorical device
into a failure.

## Counterexamples

Repetition, fragments, em dashes, passive voice, questions, triads, long
sentences, promotional energy, or metaphor can be right for a particular
speaker and surface.

## Positive action

Draft from approved evidence and reader consequence. Afterward, inspect
portable filler, vague attribution, repeated grammar, unsupported uplift, and
chatbot residue in context; preserve intentional voice and recheck every claim.

## Supports

`RISK-COPY-001`, `RISK-COPYFORM-001`

## Validation

Compare before and after copy for factual equivalence and voice retention. An
unprimed reader should identify the organization, distinction, proof, and next
action without the edit manufacturing personality or detector-targeted mess.

## Retention

Pinned public MIT-licensed practitioner guidance. Review within 90 days and
retain the non-detector, no-fabrication, and no-punctuation-ban limits.
