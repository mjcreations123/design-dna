# Audit-first methods must not turn image-to-code into a required proof branch

## Claim

Audience inference, existing-brand preservation, audit-first redesign,
responsive fallbacks, complete UI states, and readable visual-reference
analysis are useful methods when they fit the project.

## Observation

The pinned experimental MIT-licensed Taste Skill externalizes many useful
design decisions and encourages reference analysis before implementation. Its
image-to-code mode was reviewed but deliberately not adopted as a formal Design
DNA proof branch.

## Scope and limitations

Its fixed design dials, stack and font defaults, icon rules, dual-theme mandate,
hero limits, eyebrow quotas, image-generation requirements, and punctuation
bans would cause unrelated work to converge. The image-to-code mode does not
itself supply adequate accessibility, keyboard, reduced-motion, rights, or
browser-verification boundaries.

## Counterexamples

A supplied brand, quiet utility, text-led publication, or strict fidelity build
may need no generated concept image. A single theme, long hero, unfamiliar font,
or intentional eyebrow can be correct.

## Positive action

When independently justified and authorized, use a concept image as a visual
reference, not as a formal proof branch or authoritative specification. Keep
facts, rights, accessibility, behavior, code, and the final browser render as
the authoritative implementation boundary.

## Supports

`RISK-EXPLORE-001`, `RISK-FWK-001`, `RISK-REPEAT-001`

## Validation

Test a project that uses an authorized concept image as a reference and one that
does not use one. Confirm neither route creates a formal image-to-code gate and
that the same preset typography, theme, hero, labels, and punctuation do not
appear as cross-project requirements.

## Retention

Pinned public experimental MIT guidance. Review within 90 days and preserve the
selective-adoption, formal-branch rejection, and counterexample record.
